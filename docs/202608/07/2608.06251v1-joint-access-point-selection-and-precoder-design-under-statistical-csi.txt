# Joint Access Point Selection and Precoder Design under Statistical CSI 

Tim N. Faisst, Franz Weißer, and Wolfgang Utschick 

TUM School of Computation, Information and Technology, Technical University of Munich, Germany 

{timniklas.faisst, franz.weisser, utschick }@tum.de 

Abstract —This work addresses joint access point (AP) selec-tion and precoding for sum-rate maximization under statistical channel state information (CSI) in multi-AP multi-user systems. To this end, we propose two approaches. The first method is an iterative alternating optimization algorithm that updates the precoding vectors via the stochastic WMMSE (SWMMSE) algorithm and the assignment variables via a projected gradient descent step. The second method is a graph neural network (GNN)-based framework that solves the same problem in a single forward pass during inference. Building on an attention-based Edge-GNN architecture, we extend it to a multi-AP scenario, enabling the joint learning of assignment variables and precoding vectors from statistical CSI alone. Results show that the GNN outperforms the iterative algorithm across the tested signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) range and generalizes to varying numbers of users with comparable performance. Both approaches are also compared to various baseline techniques. 

Index Terms —Statistical CSI, access point selection, precod-ing, sum-rate maximization, stochastic WMMSE, graph neural networks 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Indoor environments increasingly rely on dense deploy-ments of multiple APs to provide reliable coverage and high data rates in multi-user scenarios. Users are commonly assigned to the AP with the strongest received signal. This greedy assignment can be suboptimal, particularly when users are spatially clustered. In this case, a single AP may end up serving all clustered users, while the remaining APs stay idle. To overcome this limitation, the authors in [1] formu-late a sum-rate maximization problem over the assignment variables and precoding vectors. They solve it via alternating optimization, updating the precoding vectors via the weighted minimum mean square error (WMMSE) algorithm and the assignment variables via a projected gradient descent step. However, the approach in [1] assumes perfect CSI at the APs. In contrast, the SWMMSE algorithm was developed to solve the sum-rate maximization problem under statistical CSI [2]. However, this algorithm assumes a fixed pairwise transmitter-receiver association and does not optimize the assignment of users to APs. Because WMMSE-based algorithms require multiple iter-ations to converge, deep neural network (DNN)-based al-ternatives have been proposed to reduce inference latency in precoder design. In particular, GNNs have been widely adopted for precoding in multi-user multiple-input-single-output (MU-MISO) systems [3], [4]. GNNs offer an advantage over other DNN architectures, such as multi-layer perceptrons (MLPs) or convolutional neural networks (CNNs), since they can leverage the inherent permutation equivariance property of many wireless policies. Exploiting this property allows GNNs to avoid learning a separate mapping for every possible permutation within each index set. A systematic approach to designing such GNNs for multiple different wireless policies is presented in [5]. Another advantage of GNNs is their ability to generalize to varying numbers of users, despite being trained on a single configuration [6]. Furthermore, an Edge-GNN computes the precoder as an “edge-level” task on a bipartite antenna-user graph [7]. This architecture has been shown to require fewer parameters and achieve lower inference time than vertex-based GNNs [7], [8]. Yet, the standard Edge-GNN aggregates all interfering edges with the same weight, thereby limiting its expressiveness. This is addressed in [5] by incorporating an attention mechanism into the framework, which is shown to improve generalization across varying numbers of users. However, the aforementioned works still assume knowledge of perfect CSI or a noisy estimate thereof. In [6], an attention-based Edge-GNN is designed for statistical CSI instead. All GNN-based approaches discussed above either assume a single AP or a predetermined association between users and APs, and thus do not address AP assignment. For multiple APs, the authors in [9] propose a GNN that jointly learns assignment variables and beamforming vectors. They argue that relaxing the integer assignment constraint introduces bias, resulting in suboptimal AP selections. To avoid this, they propose a Gumbel-Softmax reparameterization with a straight-through estimator to obtain a hard, integer-valued assignment. Their GNN employs a message-passing architecture without an attention mechanism and assumes perfect CSI. 

Contributions: We consider the joint sum-rate maximization problem over the assignment variables and precoding vectors, as in [1]. We extend this setting to statistical CSI. In our formulation, the objective is linear in the assignment variables, which allows us to relax them to continuous values without loss of optimality. This is in contrast to [9], where the objective is non-linear in the assignment variables.

First, we present an iterative algorithm that extends the al-ternating optimization framework of [1] to statistical CSI, incorporating the SWMMSE algorithm [2] for precoder updates.

This relaxation and the underlying vertex argument follow from [1]. A. Stochastic WMMSE for Joint AP Selection and Precoding 

For the joint optimization of the assignment variables and precoding vectors, an algorithm under perfect CSI was proposed in [1]. Under a fixed pairwise transmitter-receiver association, the SWMMSE algorithm [2] solves the sum-rate maximization problem under statistical CSI. We combine both approaches to solve the joint AP selection and precoding sum-rate maximization problem under statistical CSI. To this end, we tackle (3) by alternately optimizing over the precoding vectors and the relaxed assignment variables. The pseudocode is summarized in Algorithm 1. As input, the algorithm requires the covariance matrices 

Ck,n for each AP-user pair to sample channel realizations. Initially, the assignment of each user towards any AP is equally weighted. The algorithm consists of an outer loop with Lo

iterations, each containing an inner loop of Li iterations. The inner loop is based on the SWMMSE algorithm from [2], operating on a fixed AP selection. Note that we adapted the formulas to fit our MISO case and optimization problem. Here, uk,n ∈ C denotes the minimum mean square error (MMSE) receiver for user n served by AP k. The corre-sponding weight wk,n ∈ R≥0 is the inverse mean square error (MSE). Given the current precoders, both quantities are updated in closed form. The channel statistics accumulate in 

Sk and bk,n . Both are multiplied by a forgetting factor ρ to suppress the influence of precoding vectors computed under assignments that were still suboptimal in earlier iterations. The precoding vectors Vk = [ vk, 1, . . . , vk,N ] at each AP are then computed. Here, the optimal Lagrangian multiplier μ⋆k

is obtained via a bisection search enforcing ∥Vk∥2F = Pt,where ∥ · ∥ F denotes the Frobenius norm. After the first Li

iterations yield a well-informed precoding vector, we use an additional Li realizations to compute a rate estimate ˆrk,n 

based on these precoding vectors. Averaging over multiple channel realizations is necessary since only statistical CSI is available. Consequently, the averaged rate estimate allows for a more stable update of the assignment variables via projected gradient descent. The projection onto the simplex ˜A is done according to [12]. Note that we do not solve immediately for the globally optimal assignment variables, as this would prematurely commit to an assignment based on precoding vectors that are not yet fully optimized [1]. Finally, after Lo

iterations, the algorithm returns the assignment variables and precoding vectors for each AP-user combination.

B. Simulation Results 

For 1000 uniformly distributed user positions, the cVAE provides the channel covariance matrix to each AP. We split these positions disjointly into 800 training, 100 validation, and 

100 test positions. To train the GNN, we generate Dtrain ,u with 

D = 80000 scenarios, each containing the covariance matrices of N = 4 user positions drawn uniformly from the training positions. Our indoor environment consists of K = 2 APs, each equipped with M = 16 antennas, unless otherwise stated, and a per-AP maximum transmit power Pt = 1 . The GNN backbone comprises five hidden layers, each of dimension 

dℓ = 256 for ℓ = 1 , . . . , L − 1. After each layer, we employ a ReLU activation function and Group Normalization [14]. The hyperparameters are set to β = 0 .1/M , γ = 0 .1, and 

δ = 0.1/K . The GNN is trained for 500 epochs with a batch size of 200 , using the Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 3 × 10 −4. We use a validation dataset Dval ,u

of 1000 scenarios with N = 4 users for model checkpoint selection. For evaluation, we use separate test sets Dtest of 

1000 scenarios each. Depending on the experiment, we vary the number of users and their spatial distribution across these test sets. All algorithms are evaluated on the same test set. We observe that the initially relaxed assignment variables almost always converge towards binary values for Joint WMMSE , Joint SWMMSE , and Joint GNN . Only in spe-cial cases where a user is difficult to serve by either AP, the corresponding precoding vectors approach zero and the assignment variables for that user remain ambiguous, with 

an ≈ [0 .5, 0.5] T. Moreover, we observe that when ak,n = 0 ,the corresponding precoding vector vk,n also approaches zero, consistent with the discussion in Section II-A. Regardless, for a fair comparison we obtain the hard assignment for all users via ˆkn = arg max k∈{ 1,...,K } ak,n , before evaluating the rate. The rate is averaged across the 1000 scenarios of the corresponding test set. In Fig. 2a, we observe that Joint GNN outperforms Joint SWMMSE , especially in high-SNR regions. This is likely because the GNN learns prior information during training, which becomes particularly beneficial at high SNR, where the interference term dominates over noise and must be resolved accurately for good performance. Moreover, we observe that

Due to the uniform sampling of user positions, most assignments are trivial and thus equally well solved by a greedy assignment. To further demonstrate this, in Fig. 2b we evaluate on a test set Dtest ,c comprising clustered users. For each scenario, we draw one test position uniformly at random and sample the N users uniformly from its 5N nearest test positions. Here, a greedy AP assignment is suboptimal, since it may result in one AP supplying all users while the other remains idle. Consequently, a larger gap to Joint SWMMSE can be observed. We proposed an iterative and a GNN-based approach to solve the joint AP and precoding sum-rate maximization problem under statistical CSI. The GNN-based approach gen-eralizes across users and outperforms the iterative algorithm over the SNR range while additionally only requiring one forward pass during inference. Both approaches outperform baselines relying on a predetermined greedy or random AP selection.
